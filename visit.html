The visit — pollen loaded, carried, and not delivered

Two flowers face each other. One bee shuttles between them. The only difference between the flowers is where the anther and stigma sit on the tube wall. Watch where the pollen goes on the animal, and where the second flower reaches for it. Drag to rotate.

Placement is never an input. Where the pollen lands is computed from tube shape, body shape, and how far the animal can push in — there is no variable anywhere that sets it. Flower A is fixed; the slider moves flower B's anther and stigma together, because a species' own stigma must collect from wherever its own anther deposits. Still not modelled: carryover across many visits, packaging efficiency, last-male advantage, pollinator learning, or any reward at all. The bee's body is drawn rigid while the contact model lets it follow the tube's curve — at these curvatures the two differ by less than a grain's width.
