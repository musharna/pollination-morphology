# v2 — carryover inside the evolution loop

**Date:** 2026-08-01 · **Code:** `sim/evolve.js` (evaluators), `experiments/v2.js`
**Model version:** `a9e45d4` · **Config:** L2, 120 generations, seeds 1–3 (Round 2)
**Re-runner:** `experiments/v2-sweep.js`

> ⚠️ **CORRECTION 2026-08-25.** Three defects were found in this document by re-running it
> against the model pinned at `a9e45d4`. The **conclusion below survives all three** — every
> correction pushes toward _less_ evidence of an effect, not more — but the interval is wrong,
> the Round-3 narrative describes something that did not happen, and Round 3 does not
> reproduce. See [Correction](#correction--2026-08-25) at the end. The numbers as originally
> published are left exactly as they were.

The carryover experiment showed that pollen riding across visits **raises** how many species can
coexist — but it asked that of fixed genomes. This asks whether carryover changes what species
_become_.

Transfer model is now a pluggable **evaluator**. The loop, the mutation operator, the demography and
the arms are identical either way; only how fitness is obtained differs. `MEANFIELD` uses overlap of
anther placement with stigma placement; `CARRYOVER` counts transfer over a simulated foraging bout.
Both paired controls are re-run for the new path — L0 collapses to one species, L2 does not — because
a new transfer model is a fresh chance to invert them and the mean-field tests would not notice.

## Result: no demonstrated effect on what evolves

|                                                  | mean-field | carryover                           |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ---------- | ----------------------------------- |
| surviving species (paired, n=8, 18k-visit bouts) | reference  | **−0.75**, 95% CI **[−1.72, 0.22]** |
| mean pairwise overlap among survivors            | 0.0000     | 0.0000                              |

⚠️ The interval above is **corrected**. As published it read `[−1.56, 0.06]`, which is a
normal-approximation (z) interval; at n = 8 the correct one is Student's t with 7 df. Both
include zero, so the conclusion is unchanged.

**Carryover changes ecology but not, demonstrably, evolution.** At fixed genomes it clearly helped
(8→9 in a separated community, 3→5 in an unseparated one). Inside the loop, no effect survives a
strict measurement.

The mechanism is visible in the second row. Selection drives pairwise overlap to **zero under both
transfer models**, and carryover's benefit is precisely that a grain which misses one stigma gets
another chance — worth nothing once nothing overlaps. Selection removes the very overlap that
carryover would have rescued.

This is **not** a demonstration that the effect is absent. The point estimate stays mildly negative
and the interval is wide; separating "no effect" from "a true effect near −0.5" would need many more
replicates than eight.

## ⚠️ Three rounds of a measurement artefact impersonating a result

This is the third time in one session that a property of how finely something was measured has
presented itself as a fact about pollination, and the only reason none of them shipped is that each
had a reference with a known right answer.

**Round 1 — the first v2 run.** 3,000-visit bouts gave L2 **8.0 → 7.0**, which read as carryover
_reducing_ coexistence and reversing the fixed-genome result. Tempting and wrong.

**Round 2 — the convergence sweep.** Mean-field is deterministic and serves as the reference:

```
  mean-field           10 7 9   mean 8.7
  carryover v= 1500     9 6 5   mean 6.7
  carryover v= 3000     8 7 9   mean 8.0
  carryover v= 6000     8 7 8   mean 7.7
  carryover v=12000     9 7 8   mean 8.0
```

The shortfall at 1,500 visits is **estimator noise** — the carryover evaluator judges fitness from a
finite bout, so a species at 1% abundance is assessed on a handful of visits, and noisy fitness kills
marginal species. It disappears with budget.

**Round 3 — a paired test that cleared zero, then did not.** Same seed drives the same starting
community and the same mutations in both arms, so the only within-pair difference is the transfer
model — ⚠️ **this last clause is false, see [Correction](#correction--2026-08-25) §3**:

```
   budget   paired diffs               mean    95% CI
     6000   -2 +0 -1 -2 +2 -1 -2 -2   -1.00  [-1.98, -0.02]  excludes 0
    18000   -2 +1 -2 -2 +0 +0 +0 -1   -0.75  [-1.56,  0.06]  includes 0
```

~~At 6,000 visits the interval excluded zero **by 0.02** — exactly the profile of a result that does
not replicate. Tripling the budget shrank the estimate and the interval swallowed zero. Had the
sweep stopped at 6,000 visits, "carryover reduces coexistence by one species" would have been the
headline.~~

⚠️ **RETRACTED 2026-08-25. This never happened.** Both intervals above are normal-approximation
z-intervals, and at n = 8 the correct estimator is Student's t with 7 df. Under t the 6,000
interval is `[-2.18, +0.18]` — it does **not** exclude zero, and never did. There was no
near-miss to survive a stricter measurement. The paragraph is left in place because a document
whose whole subject is measurement artefacts impersonating results should show the fourth one it
committed, not quietly delete it.

**The general rule this session keeps paying for: when a new mechanism is added, it needs a limiting
case or a deterministic reference with a known right answer, and any effect it reports must be shown
to survive a stricter measurement before it is believed.**

## Limits

Eight replicates is thin for a difference of this size; the interval is consistent with anything from
a −1.5 species effect to none. Grooming is fixed at 0.12 and is not calibrated against measurement.
The evaluator is still adaptive dynamics — one mutant per species per generation, no standing
variation, no recombination — so nothing here speaks to speciation.

## Correction — 2026-08-25

Re-run with `experiments/v2-sweep.js`. **The conclusion above is not overturned** — every
correction here pushes toward less evidence of an effect, not more.

**1. The numbers are genuine, and the model they came from no longer exists.** No committed code
could ever produce this document's tables: an exhaustive scan of all 655 objects in the
repository finds `18000` in two Markdown blobs and nowhere else, and at the tree of `a9e45d4` —
the single commit that added both this document and `experiments/v2.js` — there was no
confidence-interval code of any kind. But the numbers are real. Against `sim/` pinned at
`a9e45d4`, at **120 generations** (the committed runner hardcodes 250) with seeds 1–3, Round 2
reproduces bit-exactly:

```
  row            published    pinned @120
  mean-field      10 7 9       10 7 9   ✅
  v= 1500          9 6 5        9 6 5   ✅
  v= 3000          8 7 9        8 7 9   ✅
  v= 6000          8 7 8        9 7 9   ❌  <- see §2
  v=12000          9 7 8        9 7 8   ✅
```

It does **not** reproduce against current code, and that is the real defect: since publication,
8 commits have changed `sim/carryover.js`, 3 `sim/evolve.js`, and 3 `packing.js`/`placement.js`
— including `0af4ba7` (recalibrating the transfer rate against 228 species) and `6b7c8e1`
(continuous overlap metric, removing the saturation mechanism). The mean-field reference drifted
too: it agrees with the pinned model on seeds 1–3 but differs at seeds 5, 7 and 8.

**2. The Round-2 table mixes two configurations.** The one row that would not reproduce at 120
generations reproduces exactly at **250** — `8 7 8` is the 250-generation carryover run. It is
printed beside a mean-field row (`10 7 9`) that is a 120-generation run. Two different run
lengths in one table, presented as a budget sweep.

**3. The paired design decays, so "the only within-pair difference is the transfer model" is
false.** `sim/evolve.js` draws the shared rng inside loops over `live` — `mutate` at :367 and the
demographic jitter at :399 — and extinction shrinks `live` at :416. The two arms therefore
consume equal numbers of draws only while their survivor counts agree, and survivor count is the
quantity being measured, so divergence is guaranteed rather than incidental. The effect is
variance inflation rather than bias, but it dissolves the precision the pairing was chosen for.

**4. Round 3 does not reproduce.** Four configurations were tried against the pinned model; the
best fits 6 of 8 paired differences. The exact configuration behind the headline table has not
been recovered — remaining candidates are generation counts strictly between 120 and 250, a
different seed set, or a different `boutSeed` scheme.

**What survives.** "No demonstrated effect on what evolves." Under the corrected t-interval both
budgets include zero, and on current code they still do — 6,000 gives `[-3.42, 0.67]` and 18,000
gives `[-1.37, 0.62]` at n = 8.
