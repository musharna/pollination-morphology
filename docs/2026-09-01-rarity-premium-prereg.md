# Pre-registration — #52: does roadmap B's assortment result rest on the per-slice rarity premium?

**Date:** 2026-09-01 · **Status:** registered, not yet run
**Written before `phenology.js` has any ablation flag** — as with
[#51](2026-08-31-empty-time-prereg.md), the predictions below are committed
before the code that could tune them exists.

## Why this run exists

[#51](2026-09-01-empty-time.md) set out to size one mechanism in the
evolving-width gradient and answered that question (empty time is ~40% of it).
Its sharpest finding arrived somewhere else — in a **control cell**. With visits
apportioned in proportion to display, the fixed-narrow arm lost a lineage in
**36 of 36 seeds**: `HELD` 0.417 / 0.250 / 0.250 → **0.000 / 0.000 / 0.000**, and
`ancVar/ancVar0` verified at exactly 0.0000 in every seed at S=8 outside the
runner.

The mechanism named there: under a constant per-slice visit budget, a plant
blooming in a sparsely-occupied slice takes a larger share of that slice's fixed
visits, so **being temporally rare pays**. That is negative frequency-dependence
generated by the allocation rule rather than by pollination. Apportioning visits
by display sets every plant's expected visits to `V · base_i` regardless of which
slice it sits in, and the premium disappears.

[#37](2026-08-25-phenology.md) is the project's **first positive result that
survives its own controls**: a narrow flowering season raises retained ancestry
variance (`HELD` +0.289 free / +0.368 linked), the supergene contributes nothing,
and the pool-size confound is excluded by the shuffled arm. It runs on the same
constant per-slice budget. **This run asks how much of it that budget is paying
for.**

## ⚠️ The premise is not an analogy — it is the same regime, and that is checked

#51's collapsing cell ran with `conserveDisplay: true`; #37 runs without it. That
difference is **inert at #37's own configuration**, and the reason is arithmetic
rather than luck. Conservation divides a plant's display by `occ[i]`, the number
of slices it is in flower for. At `WIDTH = 0.12`, `S = 8`:

| configuration   | occupancy distribution           | conservation             |
| --------------- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| **W=0.12, S=8** | **occ=0: 4.00% · occ=1: 96.00%** | **divides by 1 — inert** |
| W=0.12, S=16    | occ=1: 8% · occ=2: 92%           | live                     |
| W=0.3, S=8      | occ=2: 60% · occ=3: 40%          | live                     |
| W=1.0, S=8      | occ=8: 100%                      | live                     |

Verified byte-exactly rather than argued: one full `step` at W=0.12/S=8 is
**identical in 12/12 seeds** with the flag on and off — and, as the positive
control in the same script, **differs in 12/12** at W=0.3 where occupancy exceeds
one. A script that could only report "identical" cannot tell inert from
never-ran; this one is shown doing both.

So #51's fixed-narrow cell and #37's narrow·free arm are one configuration
(`N0=30`, `GENS=35`, `siteN=160`, `d=8`, `S=8`, `WIDTH=0.12`), not two similar
ones. **The 36/36 collapse is a direct prediction about #37, not an
extrapolation.**

## The intervention

`PH_DPV=1` adds `displayProportionalVisits: true` to every cell. The flag already
exists in `sim/ibm.js:1501` (added and guarded in #51) and apportions the **same
total** — `perSlice × (slices carrying any display)`, never the nominal
`perSlice × S` — across slices in proportion to the display each carries, by
largest remainder, consuming no random numbers.

**This is an ablation, not a fix.** A constant per-slice budget is a fixed forager
population that forages whatever is in bloom; a display-proportional budget is
foragers aggregating on floral abundance. Real pollinators sit between the two.
Nothing below claims #37 used the wrong allocation. The run measures **what #37's
positive owes to the choice.**

## Registered predictions

Baseline to reproduce first: #37's published control arm, 40 seeds (38 built),
`HELD` = 0.000 / 0.289 / 0.368 / 0.000 / 0.000 across the five cells. **If the
control arm does not reproduce those, nothing below is interpretable** and the
run reports a harness failure instead of a result.

- **P1 — the arm everything is built on.** `narrow · free` `HELD` falls from
  **0.289** to **≤ 0.079** (≤ 3 of 38 seeds).
- **P2 — the registered #37 contrast.** `H-link`, narrow+linked vs **wide+linked**
  (+0.368 [0.211, 0.526]), no longer excludes zero.
- **P3 — the attribution.** `H-pool`, narrow+free vs narrow+**SHUFFLED**
  (+0.289 [0.158, 0.447]), no longer excludes zero. This is the contrast that
  licensed "heritable temporal assortment" rather than "small mating pools", so
  it is the one whose collapse would relocate the mechanism.
- **P4 — the no-op control.** Both wide cells (`width: 1.0`) are **byte-identical**
  between arms. At width 1.0 every slice carries the same display, so
  proportional apportionment must return exactly the uniform budget. ⚠️ This
  control is only worth something because the narrow cells are cells where the
  flag is **not** a no-op — a no-op test with no such cell cannot tell "correctly
  neutral" from "never ran".

### All three outcomes are committed in advance

| outcome      | criterion                                                             | reading                                                                                                                    |
| ------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **COLLAPSE** | P1 holds **and** P3's interval spans zero                             | #37's positive rests on the rarity premium. It becomes a scope condition, not an error.                                    |
| **SURVIVES** | P3 still excludes zero with point ≥ **0.158** (#37's own lower bound) | Temporal assortment reaches placement independently of the allocation rule. #37 stands as published and is _strengthened_. |

## The mechanism statistics, and what they discriminate

P1–P3 can only say _whether_ #37 moves. They cannot say why, and three mechanisms
predict a collapse:

- **H_A — the rarity refuge.** Sparse slices pay a per-capita premium; bloom is
  heritable and lineage-associated (#37 measured `bloomLineage` 0.886 < 1, so
  relatives do flower together), so a lineage that becomes rare occupies slices
  that become sparse and is paid for it. Removing the premium removes the refuge.
- **H_B — reproductive variance.** Apportioning by display concentrates visits on
  high-display plants, raising variance in reproductive success and lowering
  effective population size, so _any_ polymorphism is lost faster — nothing
  temporal about it.
- **H_C — season collapse.** Thinly-occupied slices receive ≈0 visits, so mating
  concentrates into the few densest slices. The intervention would then be
  "shrink the realised season", which is a different manipulation from "remove a
  premium".

Registered directional, computed from state `step` already returns
(`visitsTo`, `blooms`, `visitsSpent`, `emptyVisitSlices`) plus `WIDTH`/`S`:

- **M1 — does the premium exist, and is it removed?** Correlation across plants
  between `visitsTo[i]` and the number of plants co-flowering in i's slice.
  **Control: negative** (rare pays). **Ablation: ≈ 0.** M1 is the positive control
  on the ablation itself — if it does not move, the flag did not do the thing the
  whole run is about.
- **M2 — is it frequency-dependent on lineage?** Per-capita `visitsTo` of the
  minority lineage over that of the majority, against minority frequency, over
  the early window. **H_A predicts a ratio > 1 under control that falls to ≈ 1
  under ablation.** H_B and H_C predict no such lineage-specific term.
- **M3 — the H_C guard.** Entropy of visits across occupied slices, and
  `emptyVisitSlices`. If visits concentrate into a few slices, the manipulation is
  season collapse and the result **may not be attributed to the premium alone**.
- **M4 — the H_B guard.** CV of `visitsTo` across plants. **H_B requires it to
  rise** under the ablation; H_A expects it to fall, since removing the
  density-driven component leaves only display.

## What would make this uninterpretable

- The control arm does not reproduce #37's five `HELD` values.
- **P4 fails** — a wide cell moves. The flag is then not doing what it claims.
- Generation-0 bloom statistics (`bloomAssort`, `bloomLineage`, `coflower`,
  `blooms`) differ between arms. The ablation changes how many visits a slice
  receives; it must not change **who flowers when**.
- `visitsSpent` differs between arms in any cell. That is exactly the confound
  #51 caught on its first run — the ablated arm spending the visits the other arm
  discards — and its recurrence voids the comparison.
- M3 fires: visits collapse into a few slices, or `emptyVisitSlices > 0`.

## Scope, stated before the numbers exist

Whatever happens, this is **one width, one slice count, `n=30`** — the same
scope caveat #37 carries. A collapse would say that #37's positive, _at the
configuration tested_, is underwritten by the allocation convention. It would not
say temporal assortment cannot reach placement under any allocation, and it would
not retract #37's other findings: the supergene still does nothing, and the
pool-size confound is still excluded.

⚠️ **And a collapse is not a discovery of an error.** #37 was pre-registered, ran
its controls, and reported them. Both allocations are defensible biology. What
would change is the _claim's scope_, which is the honest outcome of asking a
published positive what it rests on.
