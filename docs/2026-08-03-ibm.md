# The mating system erases an imposed split (roadmap B)

**Date:** 2026-08-03 · **Code:** `sim/ibm.js`, `experiments/ibm.js`, `tests/ibm.test.js`

Roadmap B has been asking what breaks the symmetry so a rare placement can invade. Six mechanisms
have been tested against a **proxy** — the rare-morph penalty `rare/common ~ 0.26` — in machinery
that structurally cannot speciate. This replaces the proxy with the thing itself.

## What is new, and it is one thing

`runBout` already returns `T[i][j]` = grains from plant _i_ reaching plant _j_. That is a **mating
matrix**, and it has only ever been summed into per-species fitness. Here a mother is drawn in
proportion to the pollen she received and her mate in proportion to who actually delivered it, so:

> **Parentage is decided by the placement geometry. Assortative mating is an OUTPUT, not a parameter.**

That matters because an assortment parameter would have built in the answer. Genomes are diploid with
free recombination (the conservative choice — linkage would only help divergence), expression is
additive because the 2026-08-02 hybrid work _measured_ additive shape to give blending placement, and
placement is still computed from shape by the same contact model. Nothing here can set a placement.

## The result

```
  arm                              final sep        tail    minority  spread
  placement decides mating         2.58 +/- 0.72     2.20      0.39     1.17
  random mating (null)             2.21 +/- 1.20     2.32      0.34     1.97
  two imposed optima (k=8)         2.13 +/- 0.40     2.08      0.42     0.73
```

**Neither splits.** A placement-mated population is indistinguishable from a null whose mating is
severed from placement entirely.

**The stronger half is the third row.** Fecundity selection was pointed at two _reachable_ placements
at k = 8 and the population still did not become bimodal — it **contracted**, to a spread of **0.73
against the null's 1.97**. Rewarding two placements is not enough, because whichever cluster falls
behind loses its mates. Mating is placement-mediated and therefore positively frequency-dependent, so
the mating system **erases an imposed bimodality rather than merely failing to create one**.

That is the rare-morph mate-finding problem, now shown with real inheritance, recombination and
hybrids instead of against a proxy. And it is what sympatric _Platanthera_ shows in the field, where
placement diverged inside one gene pool without ever isolating it
([detail](2026-08-03-platanthera.md)).

## ⚠️ Three controls, and two of them failed first

**The anchor gate caught a broken diagnostic before it became a headline.** My first measurement
correlated deviation against pollen **received** — the female half of fitness only — and read
**+0.149**, i.e. disruptive, contradicting the validated stabilising result. Realized parentage, which
is what actually decides representation, reads **−0.842**; the deviant third reproduces at 0.79× the
central third. The model was right and the measurement was wrong. "The IBM is disruptive" was one
unchecked number away from being written down.

**Two positive controls failed, both in the same mechanism class.**

1. `exp(k · distance-from-mean)`. Disruptive on a 1-D trait axis; **not** on a 2-D body surface, where
   "far from the mean" has no direction, so it rewards a shell and then collapses onto whichever
   extreme leads. This project has now walked into that exact trap twice — the panmictic experiment
   recorded the same thing, that on a |deviation| axis disruptive selection rises monotonically
   instead of forming an interior U.
2. Two imposed optima. Also failed to split.

The tell in both cases was the same and should have been read sooner: **the forced arm had _less_
placement spread than the null.** No genuine split does that.

**The third control changed mechanism class, and that is what made it informative.** Two failures of
one class left two readings indistinguishable — either selection cannot beat the mating system, or
the statistic cannot see a split at all — and a control that _shares a mechanism with the thing under
test cannot separate them_, because the mechanism under test is free to defeat it. So the third
control selects nothing. It hands the pipeline a population that **is** two lineages and asks only
whether the measurement can see it:

```
    seed   two lineages   one lineage   ratio
       1    36.33          2.01        18.11x
       2     0.85          2.44         0.35x
       3    59.49          2.63        22.61x
       4    26.31          2.17        12.14x
```

Up to **23×** the one-cloud baseline. Separation ~2 means one cloud; a real split reads 20–60. Seed 2
near 1× is correct rather than broken — two random lineages can land in the same place, and then
there is nothing to detect.

**So the negative is a real negative.** The measurement can see a split; these populations do not have
one. Without that control the whole run would have been unreadable, and the first two versions of it
were.

## What this does and does not establish

**Establishes:** placement-mediated mating is not merely neutral toward divergence — it is actively
restoring. Selection favouring two placements is insufficient on its own.

**Does not establish:** that no mechanism can split a population. Deception is the one mechanism
measured past parity and it is **not** wired into this model yet; that is the obvious next run, and
it now has a harness whose negatives mean something. N = 30 over 35 generations is also small — drift
is loud at that size (the mutation-off collapse ranges 1.2–3.9× across seeds), so this bounds the
effect rather than proving impossibility.

## Tests

11 new tests. The load-bearing one is the first: an IBM is the easiest place in this codebase to
break the founding constraint by accident, so it asserts that **no placement coordinate is ever
heritable** — the moment one is, "the population split on placement" becomes a statement about a gene
I added rather than about geometry. The rest pin additive expression, blending across the ±π wrap
(a plain average there would invent a third phenotype pointing opposite both parents), independent
segregation at ~0.5, and that the split statistic separates two clouds from one.
