# Sectile pollinia — the third condition, and the mechanism does not reach it

**Date:** 2026-08-03 · **Code:** `sim/carryover.js` (`sectile`, `massulae`), `experiments/sectile.js`
· Roadmap E's "sharpest remaining test".

> ⚠️⚠️ **CORRECTION 2026-08-28.** Every **`flowers serviced`** figure below is wrong: it counted
> deposition EVENTS, not distinct recipient flowers, and it is the counter the massula count was
> selected on. **The headline result survives untouched** — no removal, transfer or export figure
> changes — but the parameter selection behind it does not, and the corrected sweep is a _stronger_
> negative. See [Correction](#-correction--2026-08-28-flowers-serviced-counted-the-wrong-objects)
> at the end. The numbers as originally published are left exactly as they were.

## The test

`dispersalUnit` reproduced Johnson & Harder 2023's crossed pair — solid pollinia low removal / high
transfer, granular monads the reverse — from one mechanism, with `viscidium` selected on removal
alone so transfer was a prediction. **That is two points, and two points can be joined by anything.**

Sectile pollinia are the third: ~11% of orchid species have pollinia built of numerous **massulae**,
each holding several hundred grains, "which enables some pollen carryover between successively
visited flowers". If the mechanism is right rather than fitted, the intermediate condition should
land in the intermediate place with no parameter tuned to put it there.

## ⚠️ What the paper says — the roadmap had it half right

The roadmap recorded sectile as "grouped with monads on removal". Read against the full text
(PMC10547555), they group with monads on **both** axes:

> "the association of dispersal of solid pollinia with low pollen removal and high transfer
> efficiency, but of massula and monad dispersal with higher removal **and lower transfer
> efficiency** (figure 3a) was not expected a priori"

But figure 3 shows a two-sided structure, and that is the part worth predicting. Values read off
figure 3 — **my reading of the massula point is calibrated against two figures the text states
outright**, monad PTE 1.4% and solid-orchid 28.5%, so the axis reading is sound to about ±1%:

| unit                  | removal  | PTE       | net export | Tukey (PTE) | Tukey (export) |
| --------------------- | -------- | --------- | ---------- | ----------- | -------------- |
| monad                 | 0.83     | 1.5%      | 0.015      | a           | a              |
| **massula (sectile)** | **0.74** | **10.7%** | **0.085**  | **a**       | **b**          |
| solid, orchid         | 0.34     | 28.5%     | 0.115      | b           | b              |
| solid, milkweed       | 0.34     | 24.5%     | 0.077      | b           | b              |

`removal × PTE` reproduces the export column for every row, so the readings are internally
consistent. **On transfer efficiency massulae group with monads; on net export they group with the
solid pollinia, and monads sit alone and worst.** Sectile buys pollinium-level net export at
monad-like removal. Reproducing one grouping and not the other is not reproducing the finding.

## The implementation, and how the parameter was chosen

Sectile is the same mechanism at an intermediate grain size: a massula detaches like a monad dose
(no all-or-nothing viscidium gate, so removal stays high) but travels and lands as **one coherent
object**. `massulae` interpolates the whole way — 1 is a pollinium without the gate,
`pollenPerFlower` is granular monads.

`massulae` is selected on **carryover length**, an axis that is not transfer, so transfer stays a
prediction — the same discipline `viscidium` was chosen under. The paper states the target in text:
"one flower for many orchids with solid pollinia, two flowers for milkweeds, **a few to 20 flowers
for species with massulate pollinia**, tens of flowers for species with monads."

**Pre-registered, and it is the _position_ that was registered, not a number:** the measured massula
sits **67% of the way** from monad to solid orchid on a log PTE scale. That comes entirely from
published data. Mapped onto the model's own anchors (7.1% and 18.6%) it predicts 13.5%.

## The result — a clean negative

```
  unit          removal   transfer   net export   flowers serviced
  monad           99.9%       7.1%         7.1%                8.6
  SECTILE        100.0%       6.9%         6.9%                4.1
  solid pollinium  27.0%      18.6%         5.0%                0.1
```

**Sectile lands at 6.9%, which is −3% of the way from monad to pollinium — indistinguishable from
monads.** Predicted 67%. The export grouping fails too: sectile's net export sits with the monads,
where the measured unit sits with the pollinia. **Packaging grain size alone does not produce the
sectile economy.**

## ⚠️ The negative survives a resolution control, and that sharpens it

A negative from a 60-grain pool has an obvious alternative explanation — real massulae hold hundreds
of grains and real pollinia tens of thousands, so a coarse pool might leave no room for a massula to
be meaningfully coherent. This project has been caught by exactly that before, when a 24-bin
histogram saturated and made a real effect vanish. So the negative got the same treatment:

```
   pool   massulae   grains each   transfer   flowers serviced
     60         10           6.0      10.7%                2.1
    600         10          60.0      10.7%                2.1
   6000         10         600.0      10.7%                2.1
     60         30           2.0       6.9%                4.1
    600         30          20.0       6.9%                4.1
   6000         30         200.0       6.9%                4.1
```

**Transfer depends on the massula COUNT alone and not on the pool.** Identical at 60, 600 and 6000
grains — a hundredfold change in grains per massula moves it not at all. Grains-per-massula is
irrelevant to transfer in this model; what matters is how many separate objects ride the animal.

So the negative is not a resolution artefact. **What binds is a tension between the two axes:**
carryover length pushes the massula count up, and transfer falls as it rises. At count 10 transfer
reaches 10.7% (position 42%, much nearer the target) but only 2.1 flowers are serviced; at count 30
carryover is right and transfer collapses to monad level. **The model cannot match sectile's
carryover length and its transfer efficiency at the same time.** That is a sharper and more useful
statement than "the mechanism fails".

What it points at: the real advantage of a massula is presumably not that it is one object rather
than six, but something the model does not represent — massula-to-stigma adhesion, or the
pollinarium's bending mechanism placing successive massulae on successive stigmas. Coherence as
implemented is scale-free in grains, and the data say the real thing is not.

## ⚠️ Two harness errors, both caught by an anchor gate rather than by me

The first run of this file produced confident green checkmarks on both sections. It was void.

**The arm configurations are not interchangeable.** Two settings are properties of the dispersal unit
rather than of the bout: the monad arm carried forward uses `presentRate: 0.05` (at the default 1.0 a
flower dumps its whole pool into the first visitor, pinning removal at 100% and destroying
carryover), and a pollinarium is glued on and not collectible food, so it needs `harvest: 0` — a
point the original file records as "simply wrong biology" to get wrong. I inherited one shared
config for all three arms.

**Then I anchored on an unselected parameter.** I took `viscidium: 0.6` from the module DEFAULTS and
assumed it was the validated setting. It is not — `dispersal-unit.js` _selected_ **0.25** as the
least extreme value clearing the removal target; 0.6 gives 88.0% removal. A default is not a
finding.

The fix is structural rather than a correction: **the experiment now refuses to compute a single
sectile number until both anchors reproduce the values their own arms were validated at**, and exits
non-zero if they do not. It fired twice, correctly, before producing the result above.

## Tests

7 new tests, and the two that carry the argument are the **limits**: at `massulae = 1` deposition
must be as coarse as a pollinium's, and at `massulae = pollenPerFlower` it must collapse to one grain
per event. If those fail, "one mechanism at three grain sizes" is a story about the code rather than
a description of it, and the negative above could not be trusted for reasons unrelated to biology.

Three mutants run, all killed — forcing `perMassula` to 1, dropping `mass` from deposited massulae,
and removing the count guard.

## ⚠️⚠️ Correction — 2026-08-28: "flowers serviced" counted the wrong objects

**Every `flowers serviced` figure above is superseded, and the massula count was selected on the
wrong number.** The conclusion of this document survives — see below — but the parameter selection
that produced it does not.

`serviced` was measured as `ageOnDeposit.length / flowersUsed`: **deposition EVENTS per donor
flower**, not distinct recipient flowers. `ageOnDeposit` gets one entry per unit deposited, so two
massulae from the same donor landing on the same stigma counted as two flowers serviced when they
are one. The overcount grows with the number of units, which is exactly the end of the sweep the
massula count is chosen at.

`sim/carryover.js` now also returns `servicedPairs` — distinct (donor flower, recipient flower)
pairs, accumulated per visit. A grain's `t` is the visit it was picked up at and one visit is one
flower, so `t` identifies the donor and `v` the recipient. The two counters differ only in
distinct-versus-events, and are identical for an indivisible unit.

### What the corrected sweep says

Section A, as the experiment now prints it (corrected column, old counter beside it):

```
  massulae   grains each   removal   flowers serviced   (old: deposits)   in target?
         1          60.0    100.0%                0.2              0.2
         2          30.0    100.0%                0.4              0.4
         4          15.0    100.0%                0.7              0.9
         6          10.0    100.0%                1.0              1.3
        10           6.0    100.0%                1.3              2.1
        15           4.0    100.0%                1.5              2.9
        20           3.0    100.0%                1.5              3.5     <- old said in target
        30           2.0    100.0%                1.5              4.1     <- old said in target
        60           1.0    100.0%                1.0              4.3     <- old said in target
```

**No massula count reaches the target range at all.** The model tops out at **1.5 flowers serviced**
against Johnson & Harder's "a few to 20" — half the low end. Under the old counter three counts
qualified (20, 30 and 60), and the middle one, **massulae = 30**, is the arm every sectile number in
this document was measured on. That selection was an artefact of the miscount.

The experiment therefore now halts at its own pre-existing guard — _"NO massula count puts carryover
in the stated range. The parameter cannot be selected on this axis, so nothing below is a
prediction."_ That guard was written for this case and it fired the first time it was true.

### The finding is unchanged, and the sharper statement is stronger

Transfer is untouched: the corrected counter changes no removal, transfer or export figure, and the
result this document reports — **sectile lands at 6.9% transfer, indistinguishable from monads,
where the data put it 67% of the way to solid pollinia** — stands exactly as published.

What changes is the diagnosis in "the negative survives a resolution control". That section
concluded: _"The model cannot match sectile's carryover length and its transfer efficiency at the
same time."_ That was too generous. The model **cannot reach sectile's carryover length at any
massula count**, whatever transfer does — the tension is not between two axes, it is that one axis
is unreachable on its own. Grain packaging alone does not produce carryover of the observed length,
which points harder at the same missing mechanism this document already named: massula-to-stigma
adhesion, or a pollinarium bending so successive massulae meet successive stigmas.

### Scope of the correction

The `flowers serviced` column in the results table and in the resolution table is wrong by the same
mechanism and is **not** restated here. Regenerating those rows needs the arm-specific
configurations this document's own "two harness errors" section warns are not interchangeable, and
the experiment now stops before reaching them. The `removal`, `transfer` and `net export` columns in
both tables are unaffected.

Covered by `tests/serviced-pairs.test.js`: pairs never exceed events; they fall strictly below for
many small units; they are exactly equal for an indivisible pollinium; and the gap widens with unit
count. The equality case is the positive control — a counter that simply undercounted everything
would fail it.
