Pollination by shape

Where the pollen lands is computed, never set.

Two orchids can share a pollinator completely and still be unable to pollinate each other, because one paints its pollen on the bee's back and the other on its belly. Here, flowers and a bee are built from a handful of shape genes — tube length, curvature, throat radius, anther depth — and where they touch falls out of that geometry. Placement is never a gene. The moment it becomes one, the whole structure collapses into a model that does not need geometry at all.

So: does mechanical isolation alone build species? Mostly, in this model, it does not — and the interesting part is why each route fails.

What was found

Almost every result is a negative, which is the point: the claim is not that an effect was found, but that the instruments are good enough for the absences to mean something. The write-up leads with the numbers, their intervals, and the commit that produced each one — and with the corrections they went through on the way, including one shipped with the 1.0.0 release.

Spread a reproductive floor thinner across the population, holding the total budget exactly constant, and coexistence falls — the registered primary, −0.221, 95% CI [−0.339, −0.101]. A registered secondary goes further: at the thinnest cover coexistence drops below the arm that never had a floor at all. Unevenness is its own harm, not the absence of a help.
